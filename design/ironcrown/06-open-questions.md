# 06 — Open questions

## Decided so far

- Land and income are the game. Risk-style, turn-based, seasons.
- No action mini-games. Every contest uses one points-and-odds system with one decision.
  The tournament mechanism (blind purse or plain points) waits until you have played a prototype.
- 800x600, 256-colour VGA palette. Visuals are for later.
- England and Wales, 18 provinces, London is the crown.
- **Every province has its own lord, with cut scenes** (decided September 2026). The four playable
  earls remain the rivals for the crown; the other fourteen provinces are held at the start by
  minor lords rather than faceless levies. Each has a name, a portrait, a house and a temperament,
  and appears in cut scenes: when you take their land, when they submit or defy you, at
  tournaments, at court. This turns the "neutral levy" of the prototype into a character and
  gives the map its cast. Implications are listed under question 1.

## Questions that need answering, in the order they block work

### 1. The minor lords: what can you do with them besides conquer them?

This is the biggest open design question now. A lord in every province invites more than a
battle. Candidates, any subset:
- **Submission**: march on a minor lord with clearly superior points and they may yield without
  a fight, keeping their seat as your vassal (income to you, garrison theirs, unrest low). Cheaper
  than conquest, but a vassal can be turned by a rival's court deed.
- **Alliance and marriage**: court deeds aimed at a named minor lord rather than a random card.
- **Vassal loyalty** as a number that replaces unrest for vassal provinces.
- **Defiance**: a minor lord whose land you took can raise a revolt or flee to a rival's court.
Decide how deep this goes. The cheap version is: minor lords are the faces of neutral provinces,
they submit or fight, and that is all. The deep version is a loyalty layer across the whole map.

### 2. Cut scenes: how many, how long, and what triggers them?

The original's strength was a few painted scenes used sparingly. Decide the budget: one painted
scene per minor lord (14) plus the four earls plus around ten shared scenes (siege, tournament,
court, wedding, coronation, defeat) is about 30 paintings. Every scene must be skippable with one
press and the game must remember that. Which moments earn a scene: first meeting a lord, taking
their land, a lord submitting, a tournament, a marriage, the crown. Text length per scene: two or
three lines, in the game's voice.

### 3. Who does the player play?

Four playable earls, chosen at the start, as in the original? Or one fixed protagonist with the
other three always AI? Four playable means four starting positions to balance and four sets of
opening scenes; one protagonist means a stronger story and a quarter of the balance work. The
prototype assumes Osric of Northumbria is the player.

### 4. Campaign length and pace

Twelve years is the target and the prototype ends in four to eight. Do you want a longer, slower
build (fewer battles, more seasons of income), or the faster game the prototype currently gives?
This sets the balance runner's target before tuning starts.

### 5. What ends the game besides the crown?

Three wins now: crown, treasury, last lord. Keep all three? The treasury win rewards the builder
who never storms London; if you would rather every game end at London, cut it and make the crown
the only win.

### 6. Deeds: one a season, or more as you grow?

One deed a season keeps turns short. A larger realm could earn a second deed (say at 6 provinces)
so the late game has more to do. Or deeds could cost gold instead of being free.

### 7. Seasons and weather

Winter stops marching and halves income. Is that the right amount of rhythm, or should winter be
harsher (attrition for armies in the field) or softer (marching allowed at a cost)?

### 8. Difficulty and the AI's personality

Three named difficulties are planned. Should the four earls have fixed personalities (Aldric is
always the raider), so players learn them like characters, or should personalities be dealt at
random each game for replay?

### 9. Save and session shape

Is this a one-sitting game with an autosave, or do you expect people to play a year or two an
evening across a week? That decides how much the game must recap on load.

### 10. Wales

Keep Gwynedd and Powys as two poor hill provinces with their own lords, or cut them?

### 11. Multiplayer

Hot-seat on one machine is cheap and fits the era. Online is a server and is out unless you say
otherwise.

### 12. Music

Synthesised in-engine, CC0 loops, or commissioned? The one real cash cost in the plan.

### 13. Name

"Ironcrown" is a placeholder. With England and a cast of lords decided, a shortlist is easy.
